What Alloro sees, and what it never does.

This is a short summary of our privacy policy. The full policy controls.

What Alloro reads

  • We collect what you give us, what our websites and app record when you use them, and data from the tools a business chooses to connect.
  • When a business connects Google, we read the listing, reviews and performance data. We post the review replies, posts and listing updates that the business approves or asks us to publish.
  • Search Console access is read only. We cannot change anything in Search Console.

What Alloro never does

  • We do not sell personal information, and we do not share it for targeted advertising.
  • We do not use a business's content, its customer data or its Google data to train AI models.
  • We never use Google data to decide whether someone gets credit or a loan.

The owner stays in control

  • A business can remove Alloro's access to Google at any time, on its Google Account permissions page.
  • When the service ends, we disconnect Google within 7 business days, and delete the Google data within 30 days.
  • Anyone can ask for a copy of their information, or ask us to delete it. Section 14 of the privacy policy says how.

Free guide

The day a business changes hands.

Four things to move from the seller to the buyer. You don't need Alloro to use this.

This guide is not legal or tax advice. For your own sale, ask your lawyer or accountant.

Your Google Business Profile

Only the primary owner can transfer primary ownership. So the seller has to do it.

Google says to transfer primary ownership to the new owner. That keeps the business info, like reviews.

Steps, done by the seller:

  1. Add the buyer to the profile as an owner or manager.
  2. Go to the Business Profile.
  3. Select More, then Business Profile settings, then People and access.
  4. Select the buyer.
  5. Next to "Access", select Edit.
  6. Select Primary owner, then Save.

The 7-day wait. A new owner or manager must wait 7 days before they can manage all the features. In those 7 days, they might get an error if they try to:

  • delete or undelete the profile;
  • remove other owners or managers;
  • make themselves or someone else the primary owner.

If the new owner or manager leaves the business within the first 7 days, Google removes them from the profile. They would need to be added again.

Plan for the wait. Add the buyer well before the seller steps away.

Your website and web address

  • Check this first: log in to the company where the web address is registered. That company is called the registrar.
  • If you can't log in, or the account is in the old owner's or a web company's name, fix that before anything moves.
  • Get your own logins for the registrar and for whoever hosts the website.
  • If the business email uses the same web address, plan its handover at the same time.
  • Keep the web address and the page addresses the same for now. Changing them is a bigger move, with its own steps.

Your reviews

  • Move the Google profile, as above. Don't start a new one.
  • Find every other place the business has reviews, and get the logins for each one.
  • When you ask customers for new reviews, don't offer a reward. Google bans any reward for a review.

Your customer list

  • List every place customers are recorded: office software, a booking tool, an email list, a spreadsheet, a phone.
  • Get your own login for each one. Don't keep using the seller's.
  • Save a copy of the list on day one.
  • Before you message the list, check what the business told its customers about their details. If you're not sure, ask your lawyer.
